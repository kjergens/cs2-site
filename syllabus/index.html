Computer Science Course Syllabus, Policies, and Contract

Instructions: Read the following in its entirety, then sign in the "Contract" section (via Schoology).

Welcome! I am happy to have you in class. To make this a productive and lasting experience, there are several requirements for this course, outlined in this document. Please take your time to read through all of the course policies so that you are prepared to take this course.

Course Overview

This course introduces the fundamentals of computer science, focusing on programming in Java. You'll complete engaging projects, homework, and quizzes to track your progress.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand basic computer hardware and software concepts.
  • Master block-based coding to visualize programming logic.
  • Write, debug, and enhance simple Java programs.
  • Develop problem-solving and algorithmic thinking skills.
  • Collaborate responsibly while upholding academic integrity.

Course Requirements

There are no required materials for this course. All the class notes will be in Schoology. You may optionally take your own notes as well.

Community engagement and building is required and includes:

  • Attending and actively participating in all classes.
  • Thoughtfully completing the homework assignments, and all other assigned work with earnest effort.
  • Taking all the required quizzes and exams.

Attendance is required. You are responsible for all material presented in class. This includes important announcements that may be made only in class and not otherwise posted. You are responsible for these announcements even if you do not attend. If you do miss class, do not email the teacher asking to catch you up on what you missed — consult Schoology and a peer to fill in any gaps from when you were out.

Technical Responsibility: It is your responsibility to bring your computer and keep it in working order. You are not expected to be an expert on installing and fixing software. However, after being given set-up instructions — and especially after your system has the required tools installed and configured — it is up to you to keep it working and to promptly get help from the IT Department if your computer stops working for whatever reason. "My computer doesn't work" will not be an acceptable excuse to avoid assignments, so plan ahead!

Homework and Project Guidelines

Collaboration Policy: Students MAY collaborate with each other, with others outside the course, and even with AI tools like ChatGPT, so long as you follow these rules:

No-Copying Rule — At no time can you copy code from any source. I take this very broadly. Not only does this mean you cannot copy-paste code from anywhere, it also means that you cannot manually type code that you are looking at, nor can you manually type code that someone is telling you to type. In addition, you cannot copy code even if you then edit it, even a lot. You simply cannot copy code, full stop.

5-Minute Rule — To help prevent code copying, I have a simple rule: any time you look at 5 or more lines of code (or pseudocode or anything code-like) that is not yours (and is not from the course notes), then you must wait 5 minutes before you can edit your own code. This is a simple rule that will prevent most cases of code copying.

Debugging Rule — You are allowed to help debug other students' code, and you are allowed to have other students help debug your code. However, all the rules above apply. You are allowed to make live edits with the debugging help of a friend. But you cannot copy code. And you must obey the 5-Minute Rule if you look at their code. You may not type your friend's code for them. You may not send your code to your friend.

Ask-First Rule — If you are unsure about anything regarding any of these policies, it is always safe for you to ask about it. I am happy to discuss anything, and there is no penalty for you asking for a clarification about any of these policies.

AI Tools (like ChatGPT): Again, you are free to use AI tools, so long as you closely follow the rules above. However, to be clear:

  • You will not have access to AI tools on quizzes or tests.
  • If you rely too heavily on AI tools to complete your homework, you will not learn the material well. This will result in lower scores on quizzes and exams, and in an overall worse learning and grade outcome for you in the course.

The bottom line: while you may use AI tools, it is your responsibility to use them appropriately and effectively, and in a way that does not compromise your learning experience. If you are unsure if you are compromising your learning, it is best to simply avoid using them.

Due Dates and Late Work: Homework is due before the start of the next class, since we typically go over it together at the beginning of that class before moving to new material. Submit before that review for full credit. Homework submitted after we've reviewed it in class, but before the end of the grading quarter, is capped at 85% credit. Homework submitted after the end of that grading quarter is capped at 75% credit. There's no cutoff — always finish it, even very late, since it's still useful practice and still counts toward your grade. (This is separate from the "lowest 3 dropped" policy below — that forgives a small number of low or missing scores outright; the late caps determine what a late submission earns in the first place.)

Grading

Course Component Weight Notes
Community Building 5% For full credit, attend and actively participate in all classes. The only exemption is for approved absences, provided by your dean.
Homework (about 15–20) 15% There are frequent homework assignments, which we go over in class and are your main way to study and practice for entry tickets, quizzes, and tests. Homework is graded for accuracy — see Due Dates and Late Work above. The lowest 3 homework scores are dropped.
Quizzes (about 4) 24% Quizzes cover at most one unit.
Tests (3) 38% Tests cover multiple units, usually the three most recent. Previous quizzes and homeworks are your study guides. The last test of the course covers all the material in the course but is not weighed any differently from the other tests. The lowest test score is dropped.
Projects (2) 18% Projects give students a chance to review and enhance the skills and knowledge covered in the course. There is a project assigned at the middle of the course and one at the end.

In-class Assessments

Quizzes: Quizzes will be given once per Unit. Quizzes will be online or on paper and will involve writing code and answering written questions about code samples and concepts, without the use of other resources.

Tests: There will be 3 tests. You must take the test in-person during class time. It is your responsibility to be aware of these dates, and you should not miss class when they occur.

More on Academic Integrity

Philosophy: I begin by choosing to trust each of you individually. However, if you cheat, expect to be caught, and expect significant consequences, as outlined in the CGPS Academic Policy document, available on our Schoology page. These rules are meant to convey the spirit of our academic integrity expectations. For example, when I say "do not copy" I always mean visually, verbally, electronically, or in any other way, even if you copy and modify it. I additionally require you to understand and uphold CGPS's more general academic integrity standards. If you are unsure of something, just ask beforehand. If you do this and strive to understand the intent of this policy and exercise common sense, you have nothing to worry about.

Generally, examples of academic integrity violations include but are not limited to:

  • Copying materials (from any source) in violation of the No-Copying Rule and/or the 5-Minute Rule.
  • Posting homework, quiz, or exam problem statements or solutions online. This is strictly forbidden, regardless of if or how anyone potentially uses your posted problem statements or solutions. In particular, this effectively disallows Stack Overflow, Reddit, GitHub, and all other such sites that require that you post your question so others can see it, and also where answers to your questions may be visible to others. Do not use those sites.
  • Attempting to "hack," decompile, disrupt, or misuse the autograder, Schoology environment, or course tools.
  • Not securing access to your work, e.g., not having a password on your laptop where a friend could access your work without your knowledge.
  • Intentionally misrepresenting your work, your effort, your participation, or any other aspect of your involvement in the course, especially if this was intended to inflate your grade.
  • Showing your paper or screen to your neighbor(s) during a test or quiz.

Assessments (Quizzes and Tests): Examples of cheating on assessments include but are not limited to:

  • Referring to any external resources (people, electronics, websites, notes, books, etc.) while completing the assessment.
  • Accessing or copying any part of an answer from another student's work, even if it is very small.
  • Attempting to access any unreleased assessment outside of your assigned testing time, or outside of a secured testing environment, even if you have already taken it.
  • Saving or copying any assessment materials so they are available to anyone outside of the class.
  • Discussing any part of the assessment with anyone who hasn't taken it (or a very similar one) until it has been released publicly. For example, taking a quiz and sending a message as simple as "I wish I hadn't spent so much time studying nested loops" before the quiz is released.
  • Violating proctoring policies, like continuing to work on the assessment (even briefly) after the proctors announce the stop time, or falsifying/fabricating a tech fail.

Plagiarism Detection: Programs are naturally structured, which makes them very easy to compare. Please be aware that I have access to several sophisticated tools to find code with a high probability of cheating, in addition to my own eyes and experience. It is futile to try to disguise code that you did not write, and these efforts may increase the severity of the violation.

Penalties: Course penalties are enacted by your grade dean, and will involve a grade penalty, contacting your parents, and a written report of the incident.

Honesty: To end this section on a more positive note, you should know that I put a high premium on honesty. If you get into an academic integrity situation, then the sooner and more completely you tell the entire truth, by far the better it is for you and for everyone else involved. The bottom line: if you regret a lapse in judgment, it is always better to let me know right away, to come clean, and be honest and truthful. You will feel better about it, as will I, and it will probably result in a better outcome for you as well.

Excused Absences and Make-up Work

Due to the homework dropping policy, extensions are not generally granted for homeworks. However, for truly extreme cases (such as long-term hospitalization), extensions may be granted, with the specific appropriate accommodation determined by your dean.

Regarding entry tickets: for a dean-approved conflict, I will arrange that you take it at the earliest possible alternate time, ideally that same day or very soon after. If you don't have an excused absence and miss an entry ticket, you will not get a chance to make it up, resulting in a 0 for the entry ticket.

You should be present for all quizzes and tests. If you are not, here are the outcomes:

  • For excused absences on quiz days, the student must schedule a retake. This will be an alternate quiz. You are responsible for arranging this. If you do not arrange a retake, you will receive a 0 for the quiz.
  • For unexcused absences, the student receives a 0 on that quiz.

Contract

By signing this contract, I state that I have read and understood all of the policies above. Additionally, I commit to the following expectations to make the most of this course:

  1. Course and School Policies: I've read the CGPS Academic Integrity Policy and the Computer Science Syllabus and Course Information document. I understand that violations will result in consequences as outlined in those policies.
  2. Responsible AI Use: In accordance with the policies mentioned above, I agree to use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools only when explicitly permitted by the teacher or assignment instructions. Unless stated otherwise, AI use is prohibited.
  3. Doing My Own Work: I agree to do my assignments independently and won't ask peers for their code or specific answers. I understand this upholds academic integrity and respects my classmates by keeping collaboration fair and stress-free.
  4. Helping Others Thoughtfully: I will not share my code or answers with peers. If asked for help, I may provide guidance through tips, resource suggestions, or Socratic questioning, but I am not obligated to assist and may politely decline.
  5. Reach Out for Support: I will proactively contact the instructor if I am falling behind or need support to succeed in the course.
  6. Grow with Feedback: I agree to welcome constructive feedback, knowing it's a chance to level up my skills and learn from mistakes.
  7. Lift Others Up: I agree that when offering critiques to peers, it will be with the intent to help, maintaining respect and avoiding condescending or derogatory comments.
